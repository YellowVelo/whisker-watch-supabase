## Before You Approve This

Plain-language flags from the self-review pass:

- **This spec grew well past its original punch-list wording, on purpose, at your direction.** It started as a one-line fix (pass the screen name into the AI prompt). While investigating it, it became clear "Ask Wysker" as a whole has never had its own spec — it was built as step 5 of a navigation-refactor spec (`0023`), and the underlying chat component (`PetAIChat.jsx`) traces all the way back to the original Base44 app import, before this team's process existed. So it inherited zero product review for AI safety. This spec now covers that gap directly, per your instruction to think about the feature end-to-end.
- **A real, existing mismatch was found: the Terms of Service already promises behavior the product doesn't deliver.** `termsOfServiceContent.js`'s "Not a veterinary service" section says outright: *"The Service must never be relied upon during a veterinary emergency... The Service is not monitored in real time and cannot respond to emergencies."* Its "AI-assisted features" section says AI responses "are not veterinary advice... are not diagnoses." Today, none of that is actually enforced in the product — it exists only as a sentence the AI is *asked* to remember to include in its own reply, with nothing checking that it did. This spec is what makes the product's real behavior match what it's already telling users, in writing, that it does.
- **Keyword-based emergency detection is a real safety net, not a guarantee — this is stated plainly, not hidden.** A fixed list of emergency phrases (sourced directly from the ToS's own "Emergency situations" bullet list, so the two stay in sync) catches obvious wording instantly, before anything is even sent to the AI. But any keyword list can miss an emergency phrased in words it doesn't anticipate, and can flag something as an emergency that isn't one (e.g. "my dog was fine after his seizure medication started working" contains "seizure" but describes a resolved, non-urgent situation). **Per your explicit instruction, this is a hard stop with no bypass** — a keyword match ends that conversation turn, full stop; the owner is told to call their vet, not offered a way to push past it and get an AI answer anyway. This accepts more false-positive friction (an occasional non-emergency sentence gets redirected) in exchange for zero chance of a real emergency being answered as an ordinary chat question. The owner can still close the message and type a *new*, differently-worded message if the redirect was a false alarm — there's just no "continue anyway" action attached to the redirect itself.
- **Whether the AI itself reliably flags urgent-sounding questions it wasn't caught by keywords is not something an automated test can prove.** The Test Plan below proves the client-side code correctly *acts* on that flag when the AI sets it — it cannot prove the AI will always set it correctly for a real, novel emergency phrased in an unexpected way. That's a limitation of language models generally, not a gap in this implementation.
- **Rate limiting is a real, separate gap, deliberately not built here — per your decision.** The `ask-vet-assistant` Edge Function has no rate limiting or abuse protection at all; any logged-in user could call it as often as they want. You confirmed this should be tracked separately rather than folded into this spec, since it's a cost/abuse risk, not a pet-safety risk. Added to `docs/launch-punch-list.md` (see Repo Findings & Risks).

## Functional Requirements

Plain-language description of what "Ask Wysker" needs to actually do, beyond what it does today:

1. **The AI should know what screen the owner just came from** (the original punch-list ask). Right now the sheet shows "Tribble · Weight" in its header for the owner's benefit, but never tells the AI that — so it can only answer based on which pet, not what the owner was just looking at.

2. **Every screen of Ask Wysker should show a fixed, always-visible reminder that it's not veterinary advice** — not just text the AI is asked to remember to say, but something built into the screen itself, so it's there every time regardless of what the AI does.

3. **If an owner types something that sounds like a real emergency, Ask Wysker should immediately tell them to contact a vet or emergency hospital — without even sending the question to the AI, and with no way to push past it.** Obvious emergency wording (suspected poisoning, can't breathe, collapse, seizure, uncontrolled bleeding, can't urinate, severe trauma — the same list already named in the Terms of Service) is caught the instant the owner hits send, before any AI call happens at all. This is a hard stop, per your explicit instruction: call your vet, don't ask Wysker Watch. There is no "continue anyway" option on the redirect itself.

4. **As a backstop, the AI itself should also be asked to recognize urgency in wording the fixed list doesn't catch, and flag it — the app should then replace whatever the AI was about to say with the same hard-stop redirect**, not show the AI's own answer alongside it. The question was already sent by the time this layer catches it (the keyword list didn't), but the same principle applies once it's caught: the owner should be told to call their vet, not shown an AI-generated answer to what turned out to be a real emergency question.

5. **The AI should never sound alarmist, and should never diagnose** — this isn't new (it's already `docs/foundation/0003 Product Principles_V2.md`'s existing, locked AI Principles #12 and #14), but today it's enforced by nothing at all beyond hoping the model does the right thing. This spec makes the instruction consistent across every place the app talks to the AI, instead of three near-duplicate, slightly-different copies of similar wording.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Given the owner opens Ask Wysker from a pet's Weight screen and asks a question, when the request is sent to the AI, then it includes text indicating the owner just came from that pet's Weight screen. *(Same as the original, narrower punch-list ask — unchanged from the first draft of this spec.)*
- Given the owner opens Ask Wysker from any other recognized screen (Trends, Timeline, Food, Vet Export, Profile, or the pet's general "Overview"), when a question is asked, then the corresponding screen name is included the same way.
- Given the owner opens Ask Wysker with no specific screen (e.g. from Home, general household-wide mode), when a question is asked, then no screen text is added — unchanged from today.
- Given any screen of the Ask Wysker sheet is open (Insights tab, Ask a Question tab, or general household chat), when the owner looks at it, then a fixed, non-AI-generated disclaimer is visible stating this is general information, not veterinary advice, and to contact a vet for real concerns.
- Given the owner types a message containing wording that matches the emergency keyword list (e.g. "antifreeze," "can't breathe," "seizure," "won't stop bleeding"), when they hit send, then the message is **not** sent to the AI at all, and they immediately see a clear message telling them to contact their veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal hospital right away — with no button or action offered to bypass it and get an AI answer anyway.
- Given the owner asks a question that does **not** match the keyword list, but the AI's own response indicates the situation sounds urgent, when that happens, then the AI's actual answer is **not** shown at all — only the same hard-stop "contact your vet or nearest ER" message.
- Given the owner asks an ordinary, non-urgent question, when the AI responds, then no emergency messaging appears at all, and the experience is unchanged from today.
- Given `npm run build` and `npm run lint`, when run after this change, then both complete with no errors.

## Test Plan

- Screen name reaches the AI request (Weight screen; representative of all recognized screens) → new Playwright test: navigate to a pet's Weight screen, open Ask Wysker, use `page.route()` to intercept the `ask-vet-assistant` request, send a message, assert the intercepted request body's `prompt` contains the pet's name and "Weight." **This is the first use of Playwright's request-interception (`page.route`) in this repo's suite** — no existing test does this, so it's new infrastructure for this spec, not reuse of a proven pattern. It doesn't require a real AI call; the route can be fulfilled with a synthetic JSON response instead of letting it hit the real Anthropic API.
- Every other recognized screen name (Timeline, Food, Vet Export, Profile, Overview) and the no-screen case (Home) → **not each given its own Playwright test**, for the same reason as the original draft: `petScreenContext.test.js` already unit-tests the URL-to-screen-name mapping directly. One Playwright test proves the mapping's output actually reaches the network request; the unit test proves the mapping itself is correct for every case.
- Fixed disclaimer banner is visible → new Playwright test: open Ask Wysker in pet-scoped mode (Insights tab, default) and in general mode (from Home), assert the disclaimer text is present in both, and remains present after switching to the "Ask a Question" tab.
- Emergency keyword match blocks the AI call and shows the redirect → new Playwright test: open Ask Wysker for a pet, type a message containing an emergency keyword (e.g. "my dog got into antifreeze"), send it, assert (a) the emergency message appears in the thread, (b) no request was made to `ask-vet-assistant` for that action (assert via a request-count check on the intercepted route, or `page.route` never firing for that specific message), and (c) no bypass/continue action is present anywhere in the redirect. This is a fully deterministic assertion — no AI response needed at all, since the point is the call never happens.
- AI-flagged urgency backstop (keyword list didn't catch it, but the AI's structured response says `urgent: true`) → new Playwright test: send an ordinary-looking message, intercept the `ask-vet-assistant` request and **fulfill it with a synthetic response** (`{ reply: "...", urgent: true }`) rather than calling the real API, then assert only the hard-stop redirect renders and the AI's actual `reply` text is **not** shown anywhere on screen. This proves the client-side handling is wired correctly. **It does not and cannot prove the real Claude model will actually set `urgent: true` for a genuine, unexpectedly-worded emergency** — that's a model-behavior question, not something this suite can verify; see Repo Findings & Risks.
- Ordinary question shows no emergency messaging → new Playwright test: same fulfilled-response approach, `urgent: false`, assert no emergency UI appears.
- **Seeding/access constraints:** None — everything here is reachable via a normal signed-in session (`test1@wyskerwatch.com`) with an existing pet, no server-only or admin-only data involved, and no test needs a real Anthropic API call (all AI-response-dependent assertions use `page.route().fulfill()` with a synthetic body instead).

## Technical Spec

- **Schema:** None.
- **API / Edge Functions:** No changes needed to `supabase/functions/ask-vet-assistant/index.ts` — it's already a generic pass-through that accepts any `prompt` and optional `response_json_schema`; asking for a structured `{ reply, urgent }` response instead of plain text uses a capability it already has (the same one `PetAIInsights.jsx` and the medication/vaccination scan features already use).
- **Components/files touched:**
  - **New: `src/lib/aiGuardrails.js`** — shared module, single source of truth instead of three near-duplicate copies:
    - `EMERGENCY_KEYWORD_PATTERNS` — keyword groups sourced directly from `termsOfServiceContent.js`'s existing "Emergency situations" bullet list (difficulty breathing, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning, uncontrolled bleeding, inability to urinate, severe trauma), each expanded into real word/phrase variants an owner would actually type (e.g. "poisoning" → also matches "poison," "antifreeze," "toxic," "ate something," "ingested"). Kept as an exported, documented list specifically so it's easy to review and extend later without touching component code.
    - `detectEmergencyKeywords(text)` — case-insensitive, word-boundary matching against the above; returns the matched category or `null`. Pure function, unit-testable directly (same precedent as `petScreenContext.js`).
    - `AI_DISCLAIMER_SENTENCE` — the single shared "always recommend a licensed veterinarian" instruction, replacing the three slightly-different copies currently living separately in `PetAIChat.jsx`, `PetAIInsights.jsx`, and `GeneralAskWyskerChat.jsx`.
    - `URGENT_FLAG_INSTRUCTION` — the instruction appended to every AI-facing prompt asking it to (a) never diagnose or create unnecessary urgency (Product Principles #12/#14, quoted directly) and (b) include a boolean `urgent` field in its structured response if the question describes a real, time-sensitive situation.
    - `EMERGENCY_REDIRECT_TEXT(petName)` — the fixed, non-AI-generated message shown for both the keyword short-circuit and the AI-flagged backstop ("Please contact your veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal hospital right away" + pet-scoped or general wording depending on whether `petName` is passed).
  - **New: `src/components/AIDisclaimerBanner.jsx`** — the fixed disclaimer, extracted from `PetAIInsights.jsx`'s existing inline box so it can be reused by `AskWyskerSheet.jsx` directly (rendered once, above the Insights/Chat tab toggle, so it's visible regardless of which tab or mode is active) instead of needing its own copy per tab.
  - **New: `src/components/AIEmergencyNotice.jsx`** — the fixed, hard-stop emergency redirect message, shared by `PetAIChat.jsx`, `GeneralAskWyskerChat.jsx`, and `PetAIInsights.jsx` (all three need the identical behavior; building it once avoids drift between them). No bypass/continue action of any kind — just the redirect text and, where a pet is in context, its name.
  - `src/components/AskWyskerSheet.jsx` (~lines 100-101) — passes `screen` (already in scope, currently only used for the header display) into `PetAIChat`/`PetAIInsights`; renders `<AIDisclaimerBanner />` once above the tab toggle.
  - `src/components/PetAIChat.jsx` — `send()` (line 27) calls `detectEmergencyKeywords(text)` before doing anything else; on a match, renders `<AIEmergencyNotice>` in place of a normal AI turn and returns without calling `invokeAI` at all — the owner's message is still shown (so they can see what they asked), but no AI turn follows it, only the redirect. Otherwise proceeds as today, but now requests a structured `{ reply, urgent }` response (via `response_json_schema`) instead of plain text; if `urgent === true` comes back, the reply text is discarded and `<AIEmergencyNotice>` is rendered in its place — the AI's actual answer is never shown when this flag is set. `SYSTEM_CONTEXT` (line 6) is rebuilt from the shared `AI_DISCLAIMER_SENTENCE` + `URGENT_FLAG_INSTRUCTION` instead of its own inline copy, and gains the `screen` sentence from the original punch-list ask.
  - `src/components/PetAIInsights.jsx` — same `AI_DISCLAIMER_SENTENCE`/`URGENT_FLAG_INSTRUCTION` consolidation and `screen` sentence in `buildContext()` (line 13). Insights has no free-text user input, so the keyword pre-check doesn't apply here — but its structured response schema (already JSON, line 56) gains the same `urgent` boolean field; if set, the generated insights are discarded and `<AIEmergencyNotice>` is shown in their place, same as the chat tab.
  - `src/components/GeneralAskWyskerChat.jsx` — same keyword pre-check + structured-response + `<AIEmergencyNotice>` treatment as `PetAIChat.jsx`, with pet-agnostic wording (no single pet name to reference).
  - No changes needed to `src/lib/petScreenContext.js`, `src/lib/AskWyskerContext.jsx`, `src/components/AskWyskerAction.jsx`, or `src/components/AskWyskerRedirect.jsx` — confirmed in the original draft, still true.
  - `BloodworkSection.jsx`, `VaccinationSection.jsx`, `MedicationEntryCard.jsx`, `VaccinationEntryCard.jsx`, `trendsClient.js` are **not touched** — none of them take free-text user questions (they're structured photo-label extraction or auto-summaries of already-recorded data), so there's no emergency-phrasing surface to guard against. See Non-Goals.
- **Design System compliance:** Checked against `docs/foundation/0005 Design System.md` including its Amendments. Both new components reuse existing tone tokens (`PALETTE.red`, `bg-muted`) and existing box/icon treatments already established elsewhere — no new color, no new visual pattern. No conflict found.
- **Constraints from CLAUDE.md / locked decisions:** This spec directly implements `docs/foundation/0003 Product Principles_V2.md`'s existing AI Principles #11-14 (AI Supports Understanding, AI Does Not Diagnose, AI Should Explain Its Reasoning, AI Must Respect Emotional Context) — a locked doc this spec is following, not conflicting with. No database, no deploy-gate change.

## Repo Findings & Risks

- **Duplicate/overlapping functionality:** `PetAIChat.jsx`, `PetAIInsights.jsx`, and `GeneralAskWyskerChat.jsx` each currently define their own separate, near-identical "you are a compassionate, experienced veterinarian... always recommend a licensed vet" instruction text, and `PetAIChat.jsx`/`GeneralAskWyskerChat.jsx` also duplicate almost all of their message-list rendering JSX. This spec consolidates the instruction text (all three now read from `aiGuardrails.js`) and the emergency-notice UI (both chat components now share `AIEmergencyNotice.jsx`), but does **not** attempt a full dedup of the two chat components' rendering code beyond that — a larger refactor than this spec's scope, flagged here as a future cleanup candidate, not fixed now.
- **Technical debt nearby:** `PetAIChat.jsx` and its siblings predate this project's spec/review process entirely (traced via `git log` back to the original Base44 app import) — this is the first time any of the "Ask Wysker" AI behavior has gone through this repo's spec-writer process at all. Worth knowing if anything else feels similarly under-specified.
- **Orphaned features nearby:** None found.
- **A real, separate gap found and deliberately left out of this spec, per your decision:** `supabase/functions/ask-vet-assistant/index.ts` has no rate limiting or abuse protection of any kind beyond requiring a logged-in session — any authenticated user (test, demo, or real) could call it as many times as they want, at cost to the project (each call is a real, billed Anthropic API request). Not a pet-safety issue, so it's tracked separately: **added to `docs/launch-punch-list.md`** rather than folded into this spec.
- **Punch list / known issues in this area:** This spec resolves the punch-list item *"Ask Wysker's global sheet knows which pet you're asking about, but not which screen you opened it from"* (P4 tier) and additionally surfaces a new one for rate limiting (see above). Both should be checked off/added via a `doc-updater` pass once this ships — not part of this spec itself.

## Non-Goals

- Rate limiting or abuse protection on `ask-vet-assistant` — real, found, tracked on the punch list, deliberately not built here.
- A full dedup of `PetAIChat.jsx`/`GeneralAskWyskerChat.jsx`'s shared rendering code beyond the new `AIEmergencyNotice` component — flagged as a future candidate, not part of this spec.
- Emergency detection on `PetAIInsights.jsx`'s auto-generated analysis of already-logged data, `BloodworkSection.jsx`/`VaccinationSection.jsx`/`MedicationEntryCard.jsx`/`VaccinationEntryCard.jsx`'s photo-scanning features, or `trendsClient.js`'s auto-summary — none of these take free-text owner questions, so there's no emergency-phrasing surface for the keyword layer to guard. (Insights does still get the `urgent` structured-response backstop and the disclaimer banner, since it does generate free-form AI text, just not from a typed question.)
- Changing which screens are recognized or their display names — unchanged from the original, narrower draft of this spec.
- Changing the sheet's default tab based on screen or urgency.
- A moderation/abuse layer for what the *owner* types (e.g. blocking off-topic or inappropriate questions) — out of scope; this spec is about pet-safety guardrails, not general content moderation.
- Building general-purpose AI-response-mocking test infrastructure for this repo — the Test Plan uses Playwright's built-in `page.route().fulfill()` directly where needed, without building a reusable mocking framework around it.
- Any bypass, "continue anyway," or override action on the emergency redirect — confirmed as a hard stop with no exceptions, per your explicit instruction.
